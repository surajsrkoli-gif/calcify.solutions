📖 What is BMR and TDEE?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest to maintain vital functions (breathing, circulation, etc.). TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is BMR multiplied by your activity level — the total calories you burn in a day including exercise. This calorie calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, considered the most accurate for most individuals.

⚙️ How Calories are Calculated

Mifflin-St Jeor Formula:

For Men: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) - 5 × age(y) + 5
For Women: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) - 5 × age(y) - 161

TDEE = BMR × Activity Factor. Weight loss recommendation = TDEE - 500 kcal/day (0.5 kg/week). Muscle gain = TDEE + 500 kcal/day.

📐 Understanding Your Results

  • BMR: Calories needed for basic survival (do not eat below this without medical supervision).
  • TDEE (Maintenance): Calories to maintain current weight with your activity level.
  • Weight Loss: ~500 kcal deficit per day leads to ~0.5 kg loss per week.
  • Muscle Gain: ~500 kcal surplus plus strength training supports muscle growth.

Adjust based on your goals — a smaller deficit (250 kcal) for gradual loss, or larger surplus (500-1000) for bulking.

📜 Science Behind Calorie Tracking

The concept of calorie balance dates to the early 20th century, but the Mifflin-St Jeor equation was published in 1990 and is now the gold standard for BMR estimation. Modern nutrition science emphasizes that while calories matter, food quality, protein intake, and exercise are equally important for health and body composition.
