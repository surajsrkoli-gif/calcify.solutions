📖 What is BMR?

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body needs to perform basic life-sustaining functions like breathing, circulation, cell production, and temperature regulation — at complete rest. It is the largest component of your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE).

⚙️ How BMR is Calculated

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, considered the most accurate for general populations:

For Men: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) - 5 × age(y) + 5
For Women: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) - 5 × age(y) - 161

TDEE = BMR × Activity Factor. Weight loss recommendation = TDEE - 500 kcal/day (approx 0.5 kg/week). Muscle gain = TDEE + 500 kcal/day.

📐 Understanding Your Results

  • BMR: Minimum calories needed for survival. Eating below BMR for extended periods can be harmful.
  • TDEE (Maintenance): Total daily calories to maintain current weight with your activity level.
  • Weight Loss: Moderate deficit of 500 calories below TDEE leads to sustainable weight loss.
  • Muscle Gain: Moderate surplus of 500 calories plus resistance training supports muscle growth.

📜 The Science of Metabolism

The concept of BMR dates back to the early 20th century. The Mifflin-St Jeor equation was published in 1990 and has since become the standard for BMR estimation. Factors like muscle mass, genetics, hormones, and environment also influence metabolism beyond basic formulas.
