📖 What is Inflation?

Inflation is the general increase in prices of goods and services over time, reducing the purchasing power of money. For example, what ₹1,000 could buy today may cost ₹1,060 next year at 6% inflation. Understanding inflation is essential for financial planning, investing, and retirement saving.

⚙️ How the Inflation Calculator Works

The calculator uses the compound interest formula to project the future nominal amount needed to maintain the same purchasing power:

Future Value (Nominal) = Present Amount × (1 + r)n

Where r = inflation rate (as a decimal), n = number of years. The purchasing power (today's rupees) of the same future amount is simply the present amount adjusted back for inflation. The total loss in value represents how much of your money's real worth is eroded by inflation.

📐 Understanding Your Results

  • Future Value (Nominal): The amount of money you would need in the future to have the same purchasing power as your current amount today.
  • Purchasing Power (Today's ₹): What your current amount's real value will be after the given years of inflation — how much it will actually buy.
  • Total Loss in Value: The difference between your initial amount and its adjusted purchasing power, showing the real erosion caused by inflation.

Example: ₹1,00,000 today at 6% inflation will need ₹1,79,084 in 10 years to buy the same goods, but your ₹1,00,000 will only have the purchasing power of about ₹55,839 in today's terms.

✅ Why Use an Inflation Calculator?

  • Plan long-term financial goals (retirement, children's education) considering rising costs.
  • Evaluate investment returns: your investments must beat inflation to grow real wealth.
  • Understand why cost of living rises and adjust your budget accordingly.
  • Make informed salary negotiation or business pricing decisions.
  • Free, instant, and completely private.

📜 Inflation in India

India's inflation is measured primarily by the Consumer Price Index (CPI). Historically, retail inflation has ranged between 4% to 8% in the last two decades. The RBI targets 4% (±2%). High inflation erodes savings, but moderate inflation is considered healthy for economic growth. Using an inflation calculator helps individuals and businesses make realistic financial projections.
