📖 What is a UUID?

A UUID (Universally Unique Identifier) is a 128-bit label used to uniquely identify information in computer systems. UUIDs are standardized by the Open Software Foundation (OSF) and are part of the Distributed Computing Environment (DCE).

⚙️ UUID v4 (Random)

  • Version 4 UUIDs are generated entirely from random numbers.
  • Format: xxxxxxxx-xxxx-4xxx-yxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx where x is any hex digit and y is one of 8, 9, A, or B.
  • Probability of collision: astronomically low — 1 in 2^122 (billions of billions).
  • Ideal for distributed systems, unique IDs, and session tokens.

✅ Common Uses

  • Database primary keys (especially in distributed databases).
  • API request/response correlation IDs.
  • Session identifiers and authentication tokens.
  • File and resource naming for global uniqueness.
  • Tracking events and logs across microservices.
